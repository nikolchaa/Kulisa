The `classic-vn` template comes with these. Players find them under **Preferences → Accessibility**.

- **Self-voicing** reads lines, choices and the button in focus aloud, with the voice the player's
  system already has. Press **V** to turn it on or off at any time.
- **Text size** makes dialogue and the history larger, and the text box grows to fit.
- **Solid text box** puts text on an opaque background, for contrast.
- **Keyboard or gamepad only**: every menu and choice can be reached without a pointer.

## Describe what is on screen

Self-voicing players cannot see your art. Add lines only they hear with a character that has
`alt` set:

```kulisa
define alt = Character(nil, { alt = true })

label start:
    scene bg kitchen
    alt "A sunlit kitchen. A white kitten sits on the counter."
    "Afternoon sun had found the kitchen counter."
```

Everyone else never sees those lines, and they do not stop the story.

## Your own screens

Other templates and your own interfaces can use the same parts: `kulisa.speech.say(text)` reads
text aloud, and `kulisa.ui.focused_text()` says what the focused widget shows. See the
[API reference](https://github.com/nikolchaa/Kulisa/blob/main/docs/API.md#speech).

<Aside type="caution" title="Not done yet">
- Screen readers like NVDA or VoiceOver cannot see the interface; self-voicing takes their place.
- Windows speaks with the system's default voice, whatever the game's language.
- On Linux, self-voicing needs speech-dispatcher installed.
</Aside>
