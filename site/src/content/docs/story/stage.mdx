The stage statements say what should be seen and heard. The engine hands each one to the
game, and the game decides what it looks like. This page gives the syntax, and what the
`classic-vn` template does with it; other templates may do something else (in `text-rpg`,
`scene` sets the illustration).

## Scene

`scene` clears the stage and sets a new background:

```kulisa
scene bg station
scene bg station night with fade
scene black
```

In classic-vn, the words name an image file: `scene bg station` shows `images/bg station.png`
(or `.jpg`, `.webp`), and every character on stage leaves. `black` and `white` need no image.

## Show

`show` puts an image on stage, usually a character:

```kulisa
show mila smile
show mila smile at left
show mila surprised
show mila laughing at right with dissolve
```

- The first word is the **tag**. Showing an image with a tag that is already on stage
  replaces it, so `show mila surprised` swaps Mila's expression without moving her.
- `at` places it. Without `at`, a new image goes to the default position and a replaced one
  keeps its place.
- In classic-vn, images are found by name like scenes (`images/mila smile.png`) and stand on
  the bottom edge. The positions are `left`, `midleft`, `center`, `midright` and `right`;
  add your own in `game/config.luau`.
- An image that doesn't exist yet shows as a labelled gray box, so you can write first and
  draw later.

## Hide

`hide` takes an image off stage by its tag:

```kulisa
hide mila
hide mila with dissolve
```

## Transitions: `with`

`with` makes a change happen through a transition instead of at once. Put it on the
statement that makes the change, and only that change transitions:

```kulisa
scene bg station with fade
show mila smile at left with dissolve
```

On a line of its own, `with` transitions everything that changed since the player last
clicked, so several changes arrive together:

```kulisa
scene bg platform
show mila smile at left
show theo at right
with dissolve
```

classic-vn has `fade`, `dissolve`, `wipeleft`, `wiperight`, `wipeup`, `wipedown` and
`swirl`, defined in `game/config.luau`. A transition can also follow any grayscale image as a
mask, darkest parts first; the config shows how.

## Music and sound: `play` and `stop`

```kulisa
play music "audio/rain theme.ogg"
play music "audio/station.ogg" fadein 2
play sound "audio/door.ogg"
play sound "audio/clock.ogg" loop
play voice "voice/mila_012.ogg"
stop music fadeout 1.5
stop sound
```

There are three channels:

| Channel | Plays | Loops by default |
|---|---|---|
| `music` | one track at a time; a new one crossfades from the old | yes |
| `sound` | many at once, overlapping | no |
| `voice` | one line at a time | no |

- The file is a path from the game's folder, in quotes. WAV, OGG, MP3 and FLAC work.
- `fadein 2` fades in over two seconds; `fadeout 1.5` on `stop` fades out.
- `loop` and `noloop` override the channel's default.
- Saves and rollback remember what music was playing.

These lines are handled by the engine itself, the same in every template.

<Aside type="tip" title="On the web">
Browsers only allow sound after the player's first click, tap or key. Music started before
that begins then; the templates' title screens take care of it.
</Aside>

## Pause

`pause` waits for a click; `pause 2` waits two seconds. Handy for letting a transition or a
sound land:

```kulisa
play sound "audio/thunder.ogg"
pause 1.2
"The lights flickered."
```

## Video

`movie` plays a video over everything and waits for it. A click, tap, key or gamepad button skips it.

```kulisa
movie "movies/intro.mp4"
"Three years later."
```

Use **MP4 with H.264 video and AAC sound**, the one format every system plays. Its sound plays on
the `music` channel, so it stops the music, which stays stopped afterwards. Loading a save or rolling
back onto a `movie` plays it again from the start. Skip mode passes over videos in `classic-vn`.

To play a video on a sprite, see [`kulisa.video`](https://github.com/nikolchaa/Kulisa/blob/main/docs/API.md#video).

## Animation

A `transform` describes movement, and `animate` plays it on something shown:

```kulisa
transform bob:
    y -20 over 0.5 in_out_sine
    y 0 over 0.5 in_out_sine
    repeat

transform pop:
    scale 1.2 rotation 5 over 0.3 out_back

label start:
    show kitty happy at left
    animate kitty bob
    "Kitty bounces on the spot."
    animate kitty
    "Now she stops."
```

Each line reaches its values together, over the time you give it, with an optional ease. Without
`over`, they apply at once.

| Line | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `x 30`, `y -20` | Moves that far from where it stands. |
| `scale 1.2`, `scale_x`, `scale_y` | Times its size. |
| `rotation 15` | Turns it, in degrees. |
| `opacity 0.5` | How visible it is, from 0 to 1. |
| `pause 1` | Waits. |
| `repeat`, `repeat 3` | Last line: loops forever, or that many times. |

Eases are the same as for tweens, like `out_quad`, `in_out_sine` and `out_bounce`. Animations
are remembered in saves and rollback: a loop starts again, and a one-shot lands where it ended.

## Layered characters

Instead of drawing every combination of face and outfit, give a character a folder. In
`classic-vn`:

```text
images/kitty/base.png
images/kitty/face/default.png
images/kitty/face/happy.png
images/kitty/hat/crown.png
```

`show kitty happy crown` stacks the base, the happy face and the crown. A layer's `default.png`
shows when no look is asked for, and folders stack in name order. All the images are the size of
the base.

## Sprite sheets

An image can hold the frames of an animation. In `classic-vn`, name it in `game/config.luau`:

```luau
config.sheets = { ["kitty walk"] = { cols = 4, rows = 2, fps = 8 } }
```

Then `show kitty walk` plays `images/kitty walk.png`. Frames run across, then down.
