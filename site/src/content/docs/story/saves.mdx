## What a save keeps

A save holds where the player is in the story (including any `call`s they are inside), every
story variable, what is on stage and what music is playing. Loading puts all of it back.
Constants and helpers (`define`, top-level `lua:`) aren't saved; the story files recreate
them.

## Editing the story after release

You will fix typos and add scenes after people have saved. Kulisa is built for that.

A save remembers each position by its label and by the statement's first line. When the
game loads a save made with an older version of the story:

1. If the statement there still has the same first line, the player continues there.
2. If it moved within its label (you added lines above it), the player continues at the same
   line in its new place.
3. If it is gone or was rewritten, the player continues from the start of its label.

So:

- **Fixing typos is safe.** A player saved on the fixed line goes back to the start of that
  label, nothing worse.
- **Adding lines and scenes is safe.**
- **Renaming or removing a label** breaks saves made inside it: they fail to load, with an
  error naming the label. Keep the old label, even if all it holds is a `jump` to where the
  story went.
- **New `default` variables** start at their default value in old saves.

<Aside title="Changing what a variable means">
If a variable changes shape (a number becomes a table, two variables merge), the game's
programmer adds a migration step with `kulisa.save.migrate` and raises the save version, so
old saves are converted when they load.
</Aside>

## Rollback

Players can scroll back through the story (wheel up, Page Up, or Back in the quick menu) and
forward again. Going back undoes everything the story changed since: story variables, the
stage and the music. Choosing differently from a rolled-back menu continues the new way.

Some moments shouldn't be undone, such as a timed choice or a game the player lost. The
game's Luau can call `kulisa.story.block_rollback()` there, and the player can't roll back
past that line. A writer can do it from the story:

```kulisa
"You chose. There is no going back now."
$ kulisa.story.block_rollback()
```

## Seen lines

The game remembers every line the player has seen, across all playthroughs and saves. The
templates use it to skip only text you have already read, and to dim choices you picked
before. Editing a line's text makes it unseen again.
