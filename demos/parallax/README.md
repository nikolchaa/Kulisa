# parallax

A showcase of layered scenes, GPU particles, depth maps, post-processing and display handling,
built only on the public Kulisa API ([docs/API.md](../../docs/API.md)).

```
cargo run -p kulisa-player -- demos/parallax
cargo xtask web --game demos/parallax --serve 8080      # add ?webgl for the WebGL2 path
cargo xtask apk --game demos/parallax
```

## What is in it

- **Valley** (`lib/valley.luau`): eleven layers with their own parallax factors: a sky shader with
  a day-night cycle (sun and moon on an arc, stars, drifting clouds), three mountain ranges, painted
  forest and grass strips that tile, a cabin with lit windows. The mountains are data textures (rock
  lightness under a sun from the left and from the right, snow, outline) that
  `shaders/mountains.wgsl` colors for the time of day, lit from the side the sun or moon is on.
  Every strip has a solid fill under it, so nothing behind shows through at any aspect ratio.
- **GPU particles** (`kulisa.particles`): fireflies at night, falling leaves (a sprite sheet with
  spin), snow, chimney smoke and campfire embers. The CPU writes only new particles; the GPU moves
  them, so the stats line shows hundreds alive at a few hundredths of a millisecond of script.
- **Diorama** (`lib/diorama.luau`, `shaders/depth.wgsl`): one ray-marched picture and its exact
  depth map. The shader shifts every pixel by how near it is (steep parallax mapping with a refine
  step), so the still picture turns like a small 3D scene as the pointer or the phone moves.
- **Camera**: follows the pointer, the phone's tilt (`kulisa.sensors`), or drifts by itself.
- **Post-processing** (`lib/fx.luau`, `shaders/fx/`): bloom (a bright pass, a dual-filter blur down
  to 1/16 size and back, added to the source), tilt-shift (the same blur, masked to a band),
  grading (lift/gamma/gain, saturation, contrast, temperature; three presets), CRT and film grain.
  They run on the world layer, so the panel stays crisp, and all of it works under WebGL2.
- **Display lab** (`lib/display_lab.luau`): outlines of the design rectangle (gold), what the window
  shows (cyan) and the safe area (green), badges anchored in the safe area's corners, and a card
  with the profile, design size, scale mode, aspect limits and scale. Switch the scale mode and the
  aspect clamp while it runs; on desktop, reshape the window to 16:9, 21:9, 32:9, 4:3 or 9:16.
  Phones get a portrait design (1080×1920) from the `phone` display profile; try it on desktop
  with `KULISA_PROFILE=phone` and on the web with `?profile=phone&safe=0,40,0,30`.

## Controls

1 valley, 2 diorama, Tab hides the panel (Settings on phones), B bloom, T tilt-shift, G grading,
C CRT, N grain, D display overlay, M scale mode, A aspect clamp, F3 stats, F6-F10 window shapes.

## Art

Generated by scripts in `art/parallax` (pure Python): `diorama.py` ray-marches the diorama and its
depth map (a few minutes on all cores), `mountains.py` renders the three ranges from heightfields
(ridged multifractal noise, front-to-back column rendering, soft cast shadows; about a minute and a
half), `layers.py` paints the forest and grass strips, the cabin and the particle sprites. The sky
and the effects are WGSL. Inter is under the SIL Open Font
License (`fonts/Inter-OFL.txt`).
